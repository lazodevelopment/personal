About Roven

Job boards broke hiring. On purpose.

Somewhere along the way, applying for a job became an act of faith. You send your work history, your references, your time — into a listing that might not exist, at a company nobody verified, through a platform that gets paid either way. Most applications end in silence. Not rejection. Silence.

That's not a bug in the job board model. It is the model. When a platform earns money on postings, sponsorships, and clicks, its customer is the listing — not the hire. Ghost jobs inflate inventory. Résumé floods inflate engagement. Nobody in the transaction is paid to make sure a real person gets a real answer about a real job.

Roven is what happens when you change what the platform gets paid for.

The name is the promise

Roven — as in proven. Hiring with receipts. Every employer is identity-verified before their first post. Every listing is a confirmed open role. And every application gets a tracked outcome: employers on Roven carry a public accountability record showing how often they respond, how fast, and how many applicants they leave in the dark.

Those scores are computed from what actually happened on the platform. They can't be written by anonymous strangers, edited by the employer, or purchased from us. Reputation on Roven is earned or it doesn't exist.

The economics keep us honest

Roven is free for job seekers, permanently — candidates are never the product. Employers post, search, and message free, and pay one placement fee when a hire actually starts. We win only when the marketplace does its job. That's not a pricing strategy; it's the entire design.

Live in Phoenix

Roven is built in Phoenix and live here first — small enough to do verification properly, dense enough to prove the model with real businesses making real hires. Founding employers hire with placement fees waived while the marketplace grows.
