Couples book the Berkshires for October and then discover that an October Saturday has 11.1 hours of daylight. That is not a problem if the timeline knows it. It is a problem if the ceremony is set for four because that is when weddings start.

Our rule for an outdoor ceremony is simple: **start it two hours and forty-five minutes before sunset.** Vows take twenty to thirty minutes, family formals another thirty, and that leaves a portrait walk that lands in golden hour with the reception opening as the light goes. Move the ceremony later than that and portraits happen in the dark; earlier and you are photographing in flat, overhead light with the good hour wasted on cocktail hour.

## The three fall months, worked out

**September.** Ceremony at 4:18 PM, golden hour from 6:26 PM, sunset at 7:03 PM. Still warm most years, the hills just starting to turn, and enough evening that cocktail hour can be outside. This is the easy month.

**October.** Ceremony at 3:26 PM, golden hour from 5:33 PM, sunset at 6:11 PM. Peak color in the southern Berkshires usually lands in the second or third week, a week earlier up toward Williamstown, and it moves with the year. The schedule that works: first look at 1:00, family formals at 1:30, ceremony at 3:00, and the portrait walk at 5:30 with the light coming through the trees sideways. Dinner is indoors by candlelight because it is dark by 6:39 PM, which is the look people wanted anyway.

**November.** Ceremony at 1:46 PM. After the clocks change, sunset is 4:31 PM and the trees are bare. It is the quietest month, the estates are empty, and the low sun through leafless maples is its own thing; we just need the ceremony before two.
