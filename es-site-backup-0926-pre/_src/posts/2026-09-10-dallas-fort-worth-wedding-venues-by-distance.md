Dallas-Fort Worth is the metro we know best, and the first thing we tell couples is that "a Dallas venue" can be twelve minutes from downtown or an hour up 380. That distance shapes the whole day: how long the wedding party is in cars, whether getting-ready happens at a hotel or at the venue, and whether guests can get back to the hotel bar afterwards. So here are the sixteen venues couples ask us about most, in order of how far they actually are.

## Reading the list

Inside the loop there are only a handful of true wedding venues, and they are all different: [On The Levee](/venues/dallas-tx/on-the-levee) is the Design District event space, [The Room on Main](/venues/dallas-tx/the-room-on-main) is a sixth-floor room on Main Street, and in Fort Worth [River Ranch Stockyards](/venues/fort-worth-tx/river-ranch-stockyards), [BRIK Venue](/venues/fort-worth-tx/brik-venue) and [The 4 Eleven](/venues/fort-worth-tx/the-4-eleven) put you within ten minutes of every downtown hotel. [Fort Worth Botanic Garden](/venues/fort-worth-tx/fort-worth-botanic-garden) deserves an asterisk on its rating: those ten thousand reviews are garden visitors, not wedding clients, though the garden itself is one of the best portrait locations in the metro whether or not you marry there.

The real Dallas wedding belt is twenty to thirty-five miles north: [Knotting Hill Place](/venues/dallas-tx/knotting-hill-place) in Little Elm, [The Olana](/venues/dallas-tx/the-olana) in Hickory Creek and [The Hillside Estate](/venues/dallas-tx/the-hillside-estate) in Cross Roads sit around Lewisville Lake, and [D'Vine Grace Vineyard](/venues/dallas-tx/d-vine-grace-vineyard) is out past McKinney. These are purpose-built wedding properties, which is the whole reason couples accept the drive. [The Dallas Oasis](/venues/dallas-tx/the-dallas-oasis) in Red Oak is the south-side equivalent, and [The French Farmhouse Venue](/venues/dallas-tx/the-french-farmhouse-venue) in Collinsville is the far one, fifty miles out and worth it for couples who want the countryside to be real.

Our rule for an outdoor ceremony is simple: **start it two hours and forty-five minutes before sunset.** Vows take twenty to thirty minutes, family formals another thirty, and that leaves a portrait walk that lands in golden hour with the reception opening as the light goes. Move the ceremony later than that and portraits happen in the dark; earlier and you are photographing in flat, overhead light with the good hour wasted on cocktail hour.

For the north-of-380 estates, remember that the last dance is a forty-minute drive from a downtown hotel after a 8:36 PM June sunset; most couples on those properties block rooms in Frisco, Denton or McKinney instead. And a March wedding is the DFW sweet spot: a 4:50 PM ceremony, a 7:35 PM sunset, and weather that has not turned yet.
