Questions to ask

Questions to ask a wedding transportation company

Not just what to ask — what a good answer sounds like, and what should worry you.

All vendor questions

Transportation is the vendor couples think about last and the one most likely to strand guests. Almost every question here is about time: when the clock starts, how many runs are included, and what happens at the end of the night.

These are specific to wedding transportation. The questions worth asking every vendor — contracts, deposits, cancellation, insurance and referral fees — are on the main questions page, and you should ask those too.

The clock

When does the billable time start - at pickup, or when the vehicle leaves the depot?

What to listen forFrequently the depot, which can add an hour you did not know you were buying. This single question changes the real cost more than any other in the category.

What is the minimum, and what does overtime cost?

What to listen forThree to four hour minimums are standard regardless of journey length. Overtime is usually billed in 30 or 60-minute blocks and weddings run late, so know the rate.

How many runs are included, and what does an extra late loop cost?

What to listen forThe end-of-night return is the run guests need most and the one most often omitted from a quote.

The vehicles

Can this vehicle physically reach my venue?

What to listen forLow bridges, narrow lanes, gravel drives and tight turning circles are real constraints. A company that asks for the address and checks is one that has been caught before.

What is the backup if a vehicle breaks down?

What to listen forYou want a named alternative and a stated response time. Vintage cars in particular are single vehicles with no spare.

Can I see the actual vehicle, not a stock photo?

What to listen forAsk for a recent photograph of the specific vehicle. Fleet images are frequently newer or cleaner than what arrives.

The details that catch people

What happens if the wedding party is late coming out?

What to listen forWeddings run late and vehicles get billed by the hour. Ask whether the driver waits, when overtime starts, and who they call - you, or your coordinator. The answer should not be that they leave.

What is the policy on drinks in the vehicle?

What to listen forSome permit alcohol, some prohibit it, some charge a cleaning fee. Worth knowing before the wedding party gets in.

Who is my point of contact on the day, and will the driver have the timeline?

What to listen forThe driver should have the schedule, the addresses and a phone number that is not yours.

Common questions

What should I ask a wedding transportation company?

Ask when the billable clock starts - at pickup or when the vehicle leaves the depot - what the minimum and overtime rates are, how many runs are included, whether the vehicle can physically reach your venue, and what the backup is if it breaks down.

Do wedding cars charge from the depot?

Many do, which can add an hour of billable time before the vehicle even reaches you. It is the single question that most changes the real cost, so ask it directly.

Should we provide transport for guests at the end of the night?

The end-of-night run is the one guests need most, especially if you are serving alcohol - and it is the run most often left out of an initial quote. Ask what an extra late loop costs.
