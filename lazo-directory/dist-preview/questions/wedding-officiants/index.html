Questions to ask

Questions to ask a wedding officiant

Not just what to ask — what a good answer sounds like, and what should worry you.

All vendor questions

The officiant writes and performs the only part of the day that is legally required and emotionally central, and they are usually the cheapest vendor at the wedding. The questions worth asking are about the writing and the paperwork, in that order.

These are specific to wedding officiants. The questions worth asking every vendor — contracts, deposits, cancellation, insurance and referral fees — are on the main questions page, and you should ask those too.

The ceremony itself

Can I read a full ceremony script you have written?

What to listen forNot an outline - a complete script from a real wedding. This is the product, and it is fully inspectable in advance, which is unusual among wedding vendors. Take the opportunity.

How do you learn enough about us to write it?

What to listen forA questionnaire, then a conversation, then a draft you can edit. An officiant who needs only your names is delivering a template with your names in it.

When do we see the draft, and can we change it?

What to listen forTwo to four weeks out, with room for revision. Receiving the script for the first time at the rehearsal is too late to change anything.

Traditions and blending

Are you comfortable including our cultural or religious elements?

What to listen forIf you want a lazo, arras, handfasting, a tea ceremony or seven steps, ask whether they have performed it and how they would sequence it. Enthusiasm plus specific questions is the answer you want.

How do you handle a ceremony blending two faiths or none?

What to listen forInterfaith and secular-plus-religious ceremonies need care about wording. An experienced officiant will describe how they have navigated it rather than promising it is easy.

The legal part

Are you licensed to marry us in this county, and who files the licence?

What to listen forRequirements vary by state and occasionally by county. You want a clear yes, and a clear statement that they file it and by when. This is the one thing at a wedding that cannot be fixed afterwards.

Do you attend the rehearsal, and is it included?

What to listen forOften a separate $100-$200. Worth it if your processional is complex or if family members are participating.

What is your backup if you are ill?

What to listen forYou need a named colleague who could step in, because the wedding legally cannot proceed without a qualified officiant.

Common questions

What should I ask a wedding officiant?

Ask to read a complete ceremony script they have written, how they learn about you before writing, when you will see your draft and whether you can edit it, whether they are licensed in your county, and who files the marriage licence.

How do I know if an officiant is legally able to marry us?

Ask directly whether they are authorised in your specific county, since requirements vary by state and sometimes by county. Confirm who files the signed licence and by what deadline - it is the one part of a wedding that cannot be corrected later.

Can an officiant include our cultural traditions?

A good one will ask specific questions about how you want a lazo, las arras, handfasting or a tea ceremony sequenced. Ask whether they have performed it before, and give them the script if your family has one.
