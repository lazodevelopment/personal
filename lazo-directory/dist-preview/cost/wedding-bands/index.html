How much does a wedding band cost?

Most couples pay $2,500-$7,000. Here is what moves that number, what you get at each level, and where to spend less without regretting it.

All cost guides

Live wedding bands commonly run $2,500 to $7,000 depending on the number of musicians and how in demand they are. You are paying for something a DJ cannot produce - a room reacting to live music - and giving up range, since a band plays their book rather than any song ever recorded. Many couples split it: band for the reception, DJ or a playlist for ceremony and cocktail hour.

What you get at each price

Small ensembles

$1,200-$2,500

Three or four pieces, or a soloist with backing. Excellent for ceremony and cocktail hour, and enough for a smaller reception.

What most couples pay most couples

$2,500-$7,000

Six to nine pieces with a front vocalist, their own PA and lighting, typically three 45-minute sets with DJ'd music in between.

Premium

$7,000-$20,000+

Ten-plus pieces with horns, multiple vocalists, production and a road crew. At this level you are booking an act, and they are booked a year or more ahead.

These are what couples actually pay, not quotes we can make on a vendor's behalf. Every wedding band sets their own prices, and the ones on Lazo publish theirs where they choose to.

What actually drives the price

Number of musicians
The most direct lever - each additional player is typically $300-$600 for the night.
Reputation and demand
The best wedding bands in a market are a known quantity and priced accordingly.
Sets and hours
Three sets is standard. A fourth, or an early start for the ceremony, costs more.
Production
Larger bands bring their own sound and lighting, which is often already in the price - check, because it saves a separate rental.
Logistics
Bands need a stage area, power, parking, load-in time and a meal for each musician. Some venues charge for the space they take up.
Learning your first dance
Many bands will learn a song not in their book, sometimes for a fee, sometimes free. Ask early.

How to spend less without regretting it

Use the band where it counts
Book them for the reception only and use a DJ or a playlist for ceremony and cocktails. This is the standard way to afford live music.
Take a smaller line-up of the same band
A seven-piece version of a ten-piece band is often 30% less and, in a room of 120 people, indistinguishable.
Ask about off-peak rates
Bands discount Fridays, Sundays and winter dates as readily as venues do.
Confirm what the DJ'd breaks sound like
Some bands provide a proper DJ between sets, others hit play on a phone. It is a third of your reception either way.

When to book

Nine to fifteen months out. Good bands hold fewer dates than DJs do - there is only one of them and they cannot double-book - so this is a category where being late genuinely closes doors.

What to ask before you book

  • Can I see unedited footage from a recent wedding, not a produced showreel?
  • Which musicians in that video are contracted for my date?
  • Do you bring your own sound and lighting, and what do you need from the venue?
  • How many sets, how long, and what plays between them?
  • Will you learn our first dance song, and is there a charge?

Common questions

How much does a wedding band cost?

Live wedding bands commonly cost $2,500 to $7,000, driven mostly by the number of musicians. Small three or four-piece ensembles start around $1,200; large bands with horns and full production run $7,000 to $20,000 or more.

Band or DJ - which should we choose?

A band costs roughly three times a DJ and creates energy recorded music cannot, but plays a narrower range of songs. A DJ plays anything, exactly as people know it, and handles announcements. Booking a band for the reception and a DJ for ceremony and cocktail hour is a common compromise.

How far in advance should we book a wedding band?

Nine to fifteen months. Bands can only play one wedding per date and the well-regarded ones in each market fill early, so this is a booking worth making sooner than you think.
