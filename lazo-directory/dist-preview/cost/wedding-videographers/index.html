How much does a wedding videographer cost?

Most couples pay $1,200-$2,500. Here is what moves that number, what you get at each level, and where to spend less without regretting it.

All cost guides

Wedding films typically run $1,200 to $2,500 for core coverage - usually a highlight film of three to eight minutes plus the ceremony filmed in full. Premium packages add a longer feature edit, drone work, and same-day edits. Video is the single most common thing couples say they wish they had bought, which is worth knowing before you cut it.

What actually drives the price

Number of operators
One camera cannot be on the vows and the reaction at the same time. A second operator is typically $400-$800 and is what makes a ceremony edit feel like it was filmed rather than recorded.
Edit length and complexity
A 4-minute highlight is a different amount of work from a 45-minute feature. Colour grading and licensed music sit here too.
Audio
Good wedding film is an audio product as much as a visual one. Lapel mics on the officiant and both partners, plus a board feed at the reception, is what separates a film you rewatch from one you do not.
Drone
Usually $200-$500 where it is legal to fly. Check your venue allows it - many do not.
Delivery speed
A same-day edit shown at the reception is a premium add-on because it means an editor working on site through your wedding.

How to spend less without regretting it

Take the shorter film, not no film
If the budget forces a choice, a 3-minute highlight at $900 beats nothing at all. This is the decision couples most often revisit.
Book photo and video together
Many studios that offer both discount the pair, and a team that already works together needs less choreography on the day.
Drop the drone before you drop the second operator
Aerial footage is lovely and nobody has ever cried at it.
Ask about ceremony-and-speeches-only
Some videographers offer a documentary package with no highlight edit at a lower price. You get the whole ceremony and every toast, unedited - which is what most families actually rewatch.

When to book

Book 8-12 months out. Videographers are usually available later than photographers because fewer couples book video at all - so if you are inside six months, this is often the easier of the two to still get.

What to ask before you book

  • How is audio captured - who gets mic'd, and do you take a feed from the DJ or band?
  • Can I watch a full ceremony edit, not just a highlight reel?
  • How long until delivery, and what is the music licensing situation?
  • Do you work alongside my photographer, and have you two worked together before?
  • What is your backup plan for a failed card or camera?

Common questions

How much does a wedding videographer cost?

Most couples pay $1,200 to $2,500 for wedding videography covering a highlight film plus the full ceremony. Budget options start near $700 for a short highlight; premium packages with multiple operators, a feature-length edit and drone work run $2,500 to $6,000 or more.

Do we need both a photographer and a videographer?

They do different jobs. Photographs are how you will display the day; film is how you will hear it - the vows, the toasts, your grandmother's voice. Couples who skip video name it their most common regret, so if budget forces a choice, consider a shorter film rather than none.

How long does a wedding video take to arrive?

Six to twelve weeks is normal for a highlight film; feature-length edits often take longer. Same-day edits are a premium service delivered at the reception itself. Get the delivery date in the contract.
