How much does a wedding planner cost?

Most couples pay $800-$4,500+. Here is what moves that number, what you get at each level, and where to spend less without regretting it.

All cost guides

Wedding planning is priced in three tiers, and the words are used inconsistently across the industry. Day-of coordination commonly runs $800 to $2,000, partial planning $2,000 to $4,500, and full-service planning usually starts around $4,500 and scales with the size of the wedding. Many planners save couples more than their fee through vendor relationships and avoided mistakes - but that is an argument for hiring one, not a reason to skip comparing them.

What you get at each price

Day-of coordination

$800-$2,000

A misleading name: good coordinators start 4-6 weeks out, confirm every vendor, build the timeline, run the rehearsal, and manage the day itself. What it does not include is help choosing vendors or designing anything.

Partial planning most couples

$2,000-$4,500

Coordination plus vendor recommendations and booking help, usually starting a few months out. The right tier for couples who have a venue and a vision but not the time.

Full-service planning

$4,500-$15,000+

From engagement to send-off: budget, venue search, every vendor, design, and the day. Often priced as a percentage of total spend (10-15%) on larger weddings.

These are what couples actually pay, not quotes we can make on a vendor's behalf. Every wedding planner sets their own prices, and the ones on Lazo publish theirs where they choose to.

What actually drives the price

Scope, not hours
The gap between coordination and full planning is months of work, not a longer wedding day. Be honest about which one you actually want.
Guest count and complexity
A 200-guest wedding across two venues with a weekend of events is a different job from 80 guests in one room.
Percentage-based pricing
Common on larger weddings and worth understanding: at 12% of spend, a $90,000 wedding means a $10,800 fee. Ask whether the percentage applies to the whole budget including the venue.
Design services
Styling, mood boards, renderings and installation oversight are frequently a separate line from planning.
Team size on the day
One coordinator can run a small wedding. Two hundred guests with a complex timeline usually needs an assistant, typically $300-$600.

How to spend less without regretting it

Buy coordination even if you plan it yourself
The cheapest tier is the one almost nobody regrets. Without it, the person running your wedding is your mother, your maid of honour, or you.
Do not pay for design you already have
If you know exactly what you want, partial planning with your own aesthetic costs less than full service and gets you the same day.
Ask about a fixed fee instead of a percentage
On a larger budget, a flat fee can be substantially less than 10-15% - and it removes the awkward incentive to spend more.
Check what your venue coordinator actually does
Some venues include real coordination. Most include someone who manages the building, not your wedding. Get the distinction in writing before deciding you are covered.

When to book

Full-service planners are booked 12-18 months out, often before the venue, because helping you choose the venue is part of the job. Day-of coordinators can frequently be booked 4-6 months out, though the good ones go earlier.

What to ask before you book

  • Which tier is this, and precisely what is and is not included?
  • How many weddings do you take on my date and that weekend?
  • Do you work for me or does the venue pay you anything?
  • Who will actually be on site, and will I have met them before the day?
  • Do you take commissions or kickbacks from vendors you recommend?

Common questions

How much does a wedding planner cost?

Day-of coordination typically costs $800 to $2,000, partial planning $2,000 to $4,500, and full-service planning usually starts around $4,500 and rises with the size of the wedding. Larger weddings are often priced at 10-15% of total spend.

What is the difference between a planner and a venue coordinator?

A venue coordinator works for the venue and is responsible for the venue's obligations - the building, its staff, its timeline. A planner works for you, across every vendor and the whole day. Most couples need at least a day-of coordinator even at venues with excellent staff.

Is a wedding planner worth the money?

The strongest case is for day-of coordination, which is the cheapest tier and the one couples most regret skipping - without it, someone you love spends your wedding working. Full-service planning is worth it when you lack time rather than taste.

What does 'day-of' coordination actually include?

Despite the name, good day-of coordination usually begins 4-6 weeks before the wedding: confirming every vendor, building and distributing the timeline, running the rehearsal, and managing the wedding day itself.
