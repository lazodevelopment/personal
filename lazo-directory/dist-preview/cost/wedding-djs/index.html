How much does a wedding DJ cost?

Most couples pay $1,000-$2,000. Here is what moves that number, what you get at each level, and where to spend less without regretting it.

All cost guides

Most couples spend $1,000 to $2,000 on a wedding DJ, typically covering five to six hours, a sound system, and MC duties. The thing that separates a good wedding DJ from a bad one is not the music library - it is whether they can read a room and make announcements without making everyone wince. Ask about the MC skill at least as hard as you ask about the playlist.

What you get at each price

Under $1,000

$500-$1,000

These are what couples actually pay, not quotes we can make on a vendor's behalf. Every wedding DJ sets their own prices, and the ones on Lazo publish theirs where they choose to.

What actually drives the price

Hours and zones
Ceremony audio in one place, cocktails in another and a reception in a third means three setups, not one.
Lighting
Uplighting is commonly $300-$800; dance-floor and monogram lighting more. It changes photographs more than people expect.
Ceremony sound
Often a separate line, typically $200-$400, and worth every cent - unamplified outdoor vows are the most common audio failure at weddings.
MC involvement
Some DJs only play music. If you want announcements, introductions and flow management, confirm it is included.
Add-ons
Photo booths, cold sparks, dancing on clouds - each typically $300-$900.

How to spend less without regretting it

Pay for the MC, skip the sparks
Effects are memorable for ten seconds. A DJ who keeps the night moving is what people describe afterwards as a great wedding.
Bundle ceremony, cocktail and reception
Buying all three from one DJ is nearly always cheaper than a separate ceremony sound rental.
Trim uplighting rather than cutting it
Eight fixtures around the room does most of the work of twenty.
Do not hand a friend an aux cable
The saving is real and so is the risk: no backup, no mic technique, and nobody to tell 200 people where to go next.

When to book

Six to nine months out is typical, though in-demand DJs go a year ahead for peak Saturdays. This is one of the easier categories to fill late.

What to ask before you book

  • Will you be the DJ at my wedding, or does your company assign someone later?
  • Are you MCing as well as playing, and can I hear you on a mic?
  • How do you handle requests, and will you play from my do-not-play list under any circumstances?
  • What backup equipment do you bring?
  • Is ceremony and cocktail-hour sound included or separate?

Common questions

How much does a wedding DJ cost?

Most couples pay $1,000 to $2,000 for a wedding DJ covering five to six hours with sound and MC duties. Budget options start around $500; premium DJs with full lighting design and multiple sound zones run $2,000 to $4,000 or more.

Should we have a DJ or a live band?

A DJ costs roughly a third of a band, plays every song exactly as people know it, and handles announcements. A band brings energy nothing else matches but plays a narrower range and needs more space and breaks. Many couples book a band for the reception and a DJ for ceremony and cocktail hour.

Is uplighting worth it?

It changes how a room photographs more than almost any other rental at this price. Eight to twelve fixtures around the perimeter does most of the work; you do not need to light everything.
