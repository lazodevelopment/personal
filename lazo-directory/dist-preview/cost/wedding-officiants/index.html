How much does a wedding officiant cost?

Most couples pay $300-$800. Here is what moves that number, what you get at each level, and where to spend less without regretting it.

All cost guides

Professional wedding officiants commonly charge $300 to $800, typically including a planning meeting, a personalised ceremony script, and rehearsal attendance at the higher tiers. It is a small line in a wedding budget for the twenty minutes that everyone came to watch - and the one part of the day where a weak choice is impossible to hide.

What you get at each price

Simple ceremony

$150-$350

A short, largely standard ceremony, often with a brief phone consultation. Fine for a courthouse-style or very small wedding.

Premium

These are what couples actually pay, not quotes we can make on a vendor's behalf. Every wedding officiant sets their own prices, and the ones on Lazo publish theirs where they choose to.

What actually drives the price

Customisation
A ceremony written from interviews with both of you is a different job from a template with your names in it.
Rehearsal attendance
Frequently a separate $100-$200. Worth it if your processional is at all complicated.
Religious or cultural requirements
Interfaith, bilingual, or tradition-specific ceremonies - including a lazo, arras or veil ceremony - take more preparation.
Travel
Standard beyond a local radius.
Legal paperwork
Filing the licence is usually included, but confirm it - it is the one part that has to be right.

How to spend less without regretting it

Have someone you love get ordained
Genuinely free and often more moving, but understand what you are taking on: they will need help writing it, a rehearsal, and someone confirming your state's rules on who may solemnise.
Take the package without the rehearsal
If your ceremony is simple and your coordinator is running the rehearsal, you may not need the officiant there.
Book a newer officiant
This is a category where enthusiasm and preparation matter more than years, and where you can hear the whole product in advance.

When to book

Four to eight months out is comfortable. If you want a specific well-known officiant, or you need an interfaith or bilingual ceremony, start earlier.

What to ask before you book

  • Can I read a ceremony you have written, or watch one you have performed?
  • How much of the ceremony is written for us, and when do we see a draft?
  • Are you comfortable including our cultural or religious traditions?
  • Do you attend the rehearsal, and is that included?
  • Who files the marriage licence, and by when?

Common questions

How much does a wedding officiant cost?

Professional officiants commonly charge $300 to $800, typically including a planning meeting, a personalised ceremony script and rehearsal attendance at the higher tiers. Simple ceremonies start near $150; highly customised, bilingual or interfaith ceremonies can run $800 to $2,000.

Can a friend or family member officiate our wedding?

In most of the United States a friend can be ordained online and legally solemnise your marriage, though the rules vary by state and a few counties have additional requirements. Check your county clerk's rules first, and give them real help with writing and a rehearsal.

What should we ask a wedding officiant before booking?

Ask to read a ceremony they have written or watch one they have performed, how much is customised for you and when you will see a draft, whether they attend the rehearsal, and who is responsible for filing the marriage licence.
