How much do wedding flowers cost?

Most couples pay $1,500-$4,000. Here is what moves that number, what you get at each level, and where to spend less without regretting it.

All cost guides

Most couples budget $1,500 to $4,000 for wedding flowers, with bridal bouquets, ceremony pieces and centrepieces as the main drivers. Flowers are the category where the gap between a photograph on Pinterest and a quote is widest, because that photograph usually contains several hundred imported stems. Seasonal, locally available blooms stretch a budget further than any other decision here.

What you get at each price

Essentials only

$800-$1,500

Bridal bouquet, a few bridesmaid bouquets, boutonnieres, and simple centrepieces. Enough for the photographs that matter.

What most couples pay most couples

$1,500-$4,000

Full personal flowers, a ceremony arrangement or arch, centrepieces for every table, and some additional styling.

Premium

$4,000-$15,000+

Installations, hanging arrangements, floral arches, aisle work and full reception design, usually with a team installing on the day.

These are what couples actually pay, not quotes we can make on a vendor's behalf. Every wedding florist sets their own prices, and the ones on Lazo publish theirs where they choose to.

What actually drives the price

Seasonality and origin
Peonies in October are flown in and priced accordingly. The same look built from what is in season locally is routinely half.
Stem count, not arrangement count
A lush centrepiece may hold sixty stems and a simple one twelve. Two quotes for 'ten centrepieces' can differ threefold.
Installations
Arches, hanging florals and ceiling work carry labour, hardware and rigging on top of the flowers.
Labour and delivery
Wedding florals are made the day before and delivered and installed on the day. A significant share of the cost is people, not plants.
Repurposing
Moving ceremony arrangements to the reception is usually free or cheap and effectively doubles what you bought.
Holidays
Valentine's Day and Mother's Day move the global flower market. Weddings near them pay more.

How to spend less without regretting it

Give the florist a palette and a budget, not a photograph
Say what you want it to feel like and what you can spend. A good florist will design to the number using what is beautiful that week.
Repurpose the ceremony flowers
Arrangements from the aisle and the arch can move to the reception, the sweetheart table and the bar. Ask them to plan for it.
Vary the centrepieces
Alternate a full arrangement with a cluster of bud vases and candles. The room reads as designed and costs meaningfully less.
Spend on the bouquet
It is in more photographs than anything else you will buy from a florist.

When to book

Six to nine months out, and earlier if you want installations. Final stem counts and numbers are usually confirmed about a month before.

What to ask before you book

  • What is in season and local around my date, and what would you do with my budget?
  • Does the quote include delivery, setup, and coming back to strike?
  • Can any ceremony pieces be moved to the reception, and who moves them?
  • Who is installing on the day and how long do you need?
  • What happens if a flower I have chosen is unavailable that week?

Common questions

How much do wedding flowers cost?

Most couples budget $1,500 to $4,000 for wedding florals. Essentials-only packages start around $800; full design with installations and hanging arrangements runs $4,000 to $15,000 or more. Seasonality is the single biggest cost factor.

How can we make a wedding flower budget go further?

Give your florist a colour palette and a budget rather than a specific photograph, so they can use what is in season and local that week. Repurposing ceremony arrangements at the reception, and alternating full centrepieces with bud vases and candles, both stretch the budget without looking sparse.

Why are wedding flowers so expensive?

A large share of the cost is labour and logistics rather than the flowers themselves - arrangements are built the day before, delivered and installed on the day, and often struck afterwards. Out-of-season blooms are also flown in, which can double the stem price.
