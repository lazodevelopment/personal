How much does a wedding photographer cost?

Most couples pay $1,500-$3,500. Here is what moves that number, what you get at each level, and where to spend less without regretting it.

All cost guides

Most couples pay between $1,500 and $3,500 for wedding photography. Below that you are usually buying fewer hours or a newer shooter; above it you are buying a second photographer, an album, and someone whose calendar fills a year out. The number that actually matters is not the package price but the price per hour of coverage, because that is the line most couples end up wishing they had bought more of.

What you get at each price

Under $1,500

$800-$1,500

Typically 4-6 hours, one photographer, digital files only. Often someone in their first two or three seasons. Real work gets made at this price - but 6 hours usually means coverage starts at the ceremony, so getting ready and the end of the reception are not photographed.

Premium

$3,500-$10,000+

Full-day coverage, a second shooter as standard, engagement session, album, and often a team with an editor and an assistant. At the top of this band you are booking a name, and the calendar is the constraint, not the budget.

These are what couples actually pay, not quotes we can make on a vendor's behalf. Every wedding photographer sets their own prices, and the ones on Lazo publish theirs where they choose to.

What actually drives the price

Hours of coverage
The single biggest lever. Every extra hour is typically $250-$500, and the hours couples regret cutting are always the last ones - the dancing, the send-off, the part of the night nobody was posing for.
A second shooter
Usually $300-$600. Buys you the other angle: your partner getting ready while you get ready, the aisle reaction while the ceremony is shot from the front. On weddings over about 120 guests it stops being optional.
Experience and demand
A photographer in their eighth season prices differently from one in their second, and both may be excellent. What you pay for with experience is consistency in bad light and bad weather, and someone who has seen your venue before.
Season and day
Peak-season Saturdays cost the most because they sell out first. A Friday or Sunday in your area's off-season commonly saves 10-25% on the same photographer.
Albums and prints
A designed album is typically $600-$1,500. Many couples buy it a year later, which is fine - just confirm the price is held.
Travel
Beyond roughly an hour from their base, expect mileage, and beyond about three hours expect a night's accommodation.

How to spend less without regretting it

Buy hours, not extras
If you have to cut, cut the engagement session and the album before you cut coverage hours. You can buy an album any time; you cannot re-shoot the reception.
Move the date, not the photographer
The same photographer on a Friday, a Sunday, or in your off-season is often 10-25% less. This is the only lever that costs you nothing in quality.
Shorten the day, deliberately
A tight 6-hour timeline that starts at hair-and-makeup and ends after the first dances captures more of the wedding than 8 loose hours with a long gap in the middle. Ask your photographer to help you build it.
Skip the second shooter only if the day is small
Under about 80 guests in one venue, one strong photographer covers it. A two-location day or a big guest list is where a solo shooter starts missing things.

When to book

Photographers with strong reputations are commonly booked 9-14 months ahead, and peak-season Saturdays go first. If your date is inside six months, do not assume you have missed out - ask about waitlists and cancellations, because openings genuinely do appear.

What to ask before you book

  • Can I see two complete galleries from real weddings - not highlight selections - ideally one shot at a venue like mine?
  • Who is actually photographing my wedding, and is their name in the contract?
  • How many edited images will I receive, and by what date?
  • What happens if you are ill or have an emergency on the day?
  • Are the files delivered at full resolution, and do I have permission to print them?

Common questions

How much does a wedding photographer cost?

Most couples pay $1,500 to $3,500 for wedding photography. Under $1,500 usually means fewer hours or a newer photographer; above $3,500 typically adds a second shooter, an album, and full-day coverage. Hours of coverage is the biggest single factor in the price.

How many hours of wedding photography do we need?

Six to eight hours covers most weddings from the end of getting ready through the first dances. Eight to ten hours is more comfortable if you want detailed getting-ready coverage or a late send-off, or if your ceremony and reception are at different venues.

Is a second shooter worth it?

On weddings over roughly 120 guests, or any day with two locations, yes - it is the difference between one angle and two, and it is the only way to photograph both partners getting ready. For a small single-venue wedding, one experienced photographer is usually enough.

How far in advance should we book a wedding photographer?

Nine to fourteen months ahead is typical, and sooner for a peak-season Saturday. Inside six months, ask about cancellations rather than assuming you are too late.
