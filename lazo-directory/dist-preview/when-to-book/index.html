When to book every wedding vendor

The order matters more than the dates. Here is what genuinely sells out, what can wait, and what you cannot book until something else is signed.

Almost every wedding booking timeline you will read is a list of months. The more useful thing is the order, because several of these decisions are locked by earlier ones: you cannot book a photographer before you have a date, and you do not have a date until you have a venue.

So the real sequence is short. Venue first, because it sets the date. Then the vendors who can only do one wedding a day and are booked by reputation — photographer, planner, band. Then everything that scales, which is most of the rest.

The only three rules that matter

  • One-per-day vendors go first. A photographer, a band, a planner and an officiant can each do exactly one wedding on your date. A florist, a caterer and a stationer can serve several. That distinction, not the category, is what determines urgency.
  • Peak Saturdays are the constraint, not the year. Twelve months out for a June Saturday is late in a busy market. Eight months out for a Friday in February is early. Adjust everything below for your date.
  • Being late is not the same as being locked out. Cancellations happen constantly, and vendors keep waitlists. If your date is inside six months, ask rather than assuming.

Book first — these sell out

One wedding per day each, and chosen by reputation. If your date is a peak Saturday, these are the bookings that decide whether you get your first choice or your fourth.

12-18 months out

Wedding Venues $3,000-$15,000+

Venues are booked first and set your date, so start 12-18 months out - and understand that for a peak Saturday you are choosing between available dates, not choosing a date and then a venue. Everything else in this guide follows from what you sign here.

9-14 months out

Wedding Photographers $1,500-$3,500

Photographers with strong reputations are commonly booked 9-14 months ahead, and peak-season Saturdays go first. If your date is inside six months, do not assume you have missed out - ask about waitlists and cancellations, because openings genuinely do appear.

12-18 months out

Wedding Planners $800-$4,500+

Full-service planners are booked 12-18 months out, often before the venue, because helping you choose the venue is part of the job. Day-of coordinators can frequently be booked 4-6 months out, though the good ones go earlier.

Book 8-12 months out. Videographers are usually available later than photographers because fewer couples book video at all - so if you are inside six months, this is often the easier of the two to still get.

Wedding Officiants $300-$800

Four to eight months out is comfortable. If you want a specific well-known officiant, or you need an interfaith or bilingual ceremony, start earlier.

9-15 months out

Live Wedding Bands $2,500-$7,000

Nine to fifteen months out. Good bands hold fewer dates than DJs do - there is only one of them and they cannot double-book - so this is a category where being late genuinely closes doors.

6-9 months out

Wedding DJs $1,000-$2,000

Six to nine months out is typical, though in-demand DJs go a year ahead for peak Saturdays. This is one of the easier categories to fill late.

Day-Of Coordinators $800-$2,000

Four to eight months out. Coordinators book later than full-service planners, but the well-regarded ones in each market still fill peak Saturdays a year ahead.

Then everything else

These scale — a florist or caterer can take several weddings on one date — so being a few months later costs you far less.

Book 8-12 months out, or immediately after the venue if the venue requires a caterer from an approved list. Tastings usually happen 3-6 months before the wedding.

Four to eight months out, and earlier for peak Saturdays or if you need a specific vintage vehicle - most operators own only one.

Start 4-5 months before the wedding. Invitations go out 6-8 weeks ahead (three months for destination weddings), and save-the-dates six to eight months ahead.

If you are already inside six months

This is a more common position than the wedding industry likes to admit, and it is workable. What changes is the order of operations: availability now leads, and preference follows.

  • Ask about the date, not the package. Lead with your date in the first message. It saves everyone the conversation that ends in "unfortunately".
  • Ask about cancellations explicitly. Vendors hold waitlists and dates genuinely free up. This is the single most useful question available to a late booker.
  • Move off Saturday if you can. A Friday or Sunday transforms availability across every category at once, and usually costs 20–40% less.
  • Book the one-per-day vendors this week. Venue, photographer, officiant, band. The rest can wait another fortnight without much cost.

What you cannot rush

A few things have a fixed lead time regardless of how organised you are. Invitations need four to five months from design to mailbox. Alterations need six to eight weeks and several fittings. A ketubah or a made-to-order stationery suite is lettered by hand. Ordering these late does not cost more; it simply does not work.
