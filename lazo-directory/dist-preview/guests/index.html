For wedding guests

You're invited. Now what?

Straight answers to what guests actually worry about — what to wear, what to give, and what you're allowed to do. No hedging, no etiquette lecture.

Nearly everything written about weddings is written for the couple. But a wedding has one couple and a hundred and forty guests, and the guests are the ones lying awake wondering whether their dress is too white.

So these are for you. Every page starts with the answer — yes, no, or it depends and here's on what — and explains itself underneath, for when you want to know why.

What to wear

What should you wear to a wedding? Follow the dress code on the invitation, and if there isn't one, assume cocktail attire: a knee-length or midi dress, or a suit and tie. Is it rude to wear black to a wedding? No. Is it rude to wear white to a wedding? Yes — don't. Is it rude to wear red to a wedding? At a Western wedding, no — red is fine, though it is attention-getting and worth a moment's thought. What does black tie optional mean? Black tie optional means a tuxedo is welcome but a dark suit and tie is equally correct, and for women a floor-length gown, a formal midi or dressy separates all work.

Gifts

How much should you give for a wedding gift? Most guests give between $75 and $200, with $100–$150 the most common single figure. Do you have to buy off the wedding registry? No, but you probably should.

At the wedding

Is it rude to leave a wedding early? Not if you stay past the key moments and say goodbye properly. What are the rules for RSVPing to a wedding? Reply by the date on the card, reply even if it's a no, and only bring the people named on the invitation. What are the rules for wedding guests? Reply on time, dress to the code, don't wear white, arrive early, put your phone away during the ceremony, don't propose or announce anything, and say goodbye before you leave.

The three that matter most

  • Don't wear white. It is the one guest rule that genuinely still holds, and it includes ivory, cream, champagne and anything that photographs white under a flash.
  • RSVP by the date, even if it's a no. About a fifth of guests miss it at every wedding, which means the couple spends a week chasing people while finalising catering numbers.
  • Bring only the people named on the invitation. Venues have hard capacity limits and catering is charged per head. An extra guest is a real cost, not a formality.

If the wedding isn't a Western one

A lot of guest advice assumes a white dress, a church and a seated dinner. If you have been invited to a Chinese, Indian, Nigerian, Jewish, Persian or any other wedding whose customs you do not know, the colours to avoid and the things that will happen are genuinely different — red is the bride's colour at some, white means mourning at others.
