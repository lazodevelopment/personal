What every couple asks about the license

How much does a marriage license cost?

Between about $24 and $125 depending on the state and county. Most fall between $30 and $90. Several states cut the fee by $40 to $75 if you complete a short premarital course, and Texas waives its waiting period for the same course.

Where do you get a marriage license?

From a local government office, usually the county clerk, clerk of court, register of deeds or probate court, and in New England the town or city clerk. In most states any county's office works and the license is valid statewide. In some, you must apply where you live or where the ceremony is; your state's page says which.

How long before the wedding should we get it?

Inside the validity window and outside any waiting period. Most licenses last 30 to 90 days; a few last 10 to 20 days, and some a year. Waiting periods run from none to three days. For most couples that means applying two to four weeks before. Each state page works out your window from your date.

Do we need witnesses?

About half the states require one or two witnesses over 18 to sign at the ceremony. The other half need only the officiant. Colorado and Pennsylvania even let you marry without an officiant.

Do we both need to be there to apply?

Almost always yes, in person with government photo ID, and in a few states a certified birth certificate. Some states let one partner apply with a notarized affidavit from the other. Many now take the application online, with one in-person visit to be issued.
