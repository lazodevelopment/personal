The first-apartment checklist.

Everything nobody tells you before your first lease — budgeting reality, application paperwork, move-in protection, and the first-week setup.

By the LeaseReputation team · Updated 2026-07-18

Before you tour: the honest budget

  • The classic rule is rent ≤ 30% of gross income — and most properties enforce a version of it, requiring documented income of 2.5–3× rent to approve you.
  • Budget the real monthly: rent + mandatory fees + utilities (ask the property for average bills — they know) + internet + renter's insurance ($10–25/mo) + parking.
  • Budget the move-in wall: first month + deposit + application/admin fees + moving costs. It's commonly 2.5–3× a month's rent, due at once.

The application packet (have it ready before you tour)

  • Photo ID, recent pay stubs (or offer letter), bank statements, prior landlord reference if you have one
  • No rental history? Expect to need a co-signer/guarantor (usually required to show 5–6× rent in income) or a larger deposit. Line yours up in advance — units don't wait.
  • Credit check happens on nearly every application; know your score going in.

Before you sign

  • Tour with our 15 questions and check the lease against the nine red flags.
  • Read verified resident reviews — the tour shows you the pool; residents tell you about the maintenance response time.
  • Get every promise in writing. "We'll replace that carpet before move-in" is worth nothing verbally.

Move-in day: one hour of documentation

  • Photograph and video every room before furniture arrives — this hour is what gets your deposit back in a year (full playbook: the deposit guide).
  • Complete the condition report in obsessive detail and email it in.
  • Test everything: every burner, faucet, outlet, lock, window, the AC and heat both. Submit maintenance requests for failures on day one, in writing.

The first week

  • Utilities in your name (electric/gas often required before keys), internet scheduled, renter's insurance active from day one — most leases require it, and it's the cheapest insurance you'll ever carry.
  • Change of address with USPS, driver's license update, find the water shutoff and breaker panel before you need them at midnight.
  • Meet the maintenance team. Being a known, polite human moves your requests up every queue in the building.

Twelve months from now, you'll know things about this building nobody told you. That's exactly when your verified review matters — write the one you wish you'd been able to read.
