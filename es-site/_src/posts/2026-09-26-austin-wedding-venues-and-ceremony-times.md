Austin has two wedding seasons, spring and fall, and one problem in between: from June through August the sun sets after 8:30 and the temperature at ceremony time is in the high nineties. The timeline for an Austin wedding is really a negotiation between light and heat, and this post gives you the numbers for both sides.

## The light in Austin

Two things Texas does differently from the Northeast. Daylight swings less across the year, only four hours between June and December against six in New England, so a winter Austin wedding is far more workable than a winter Boston one. And the March jump is the clocks: a 3:34 February ceremony becomes a 4:54 March ceremony overnight.

## The summer problem

A June ceremony at 5:49 puts guests in direct sun at the hottest hour of the day. There are three ways out.

1. **Start at 6:30 or 7:00 anyway.** Portraits happen right at sunset, family formals are quick, and the reception runs late. This is what most Austin couples do, and it works if the ceremony site has shade for guests.
2. **Move the ceremony indoors or under cover** and step out at 8:00 for a fifteen-minute portrait walk in the last light.
3. **Pick October.** A 4:15 ceremony, 80 degrees, and the best light of the year. This is why October Saturdays in the Hill Country book out eighteen months ahead.

Ma Maison is the one couples name first, a French-styled estate on the Dripping Springs side with a real lawn and a real sunset. Barr Mansion is a historic house and organic farm in the north of the city, and its ballroom solves the summer problem outright. Allan House is the downtown choice, a Victorian house with a garden, and one of the few places where you can have a garden ceremony and walk to a hotel. Ranch Austin, with the highest rating on the list, is a working ranch in the south of the city.

## What we would do

For October: ceremony at 4:15, family formals at the site, and a walk into the field at 6:30. For a summer wedding: shade or indoors for the ceremony at 6:30, a first look at 4:30 in the air conditioning, and the two of you outside alone at 8:00 for the last fifteen minutes of light. For December: a 2:45 ceremony sounds early until you see the portraits at five.
